Which statistical test should I use?

Answer up to three questions and get the matching test, its assumptions, how to report it, and the R function. A free, printable cheat sheet for thesis students.

1. What kind of outcome are you measuring?

Your main (dependent) variable.

2. What do you want to do?

Your comparison or goal.

3. Is the number roughly normally distributed?

Look at a histogram or run a normality test (for example Shapiro-Wilk). With about 30 or more per group and no extreme outliers, "yes" is usually reasonable. For paired data, check the differences.

Always: decide your test before you look at the results, state it in your protocol, and report the effect size with a 95% confidence interval as well as the p-value. If your design is not listed (for example repeated measurements over time, clustered data, or several outcomes), ask a statistician.
