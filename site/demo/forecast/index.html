How to read this

  • Error is mean absolute percentage error (MAPE) of the weekly price, over rolling origins every four weeks across the last three years. Lower is better.
  • The baselines matter. "Naive" repeats the last price. "Seasonal naive" repeats the price from a year earlier. A model that cannot beat naive has no business being used.
  • No peeking. Each backtest forecast is built only from data available at its origin. Interval bands come from past forecast errors, and their coverage is checked on origins that were not used to build them. A well-calibrated 80% band should cover about 80%.
  • Expect modest gains. Mandi prices are volatile and close to a random walk at short horizons. The method adds a damped trend and a shrunken same-season price move from previous years. It helps most at longer horizons, and it is wrong often. Treat the bands, not the line, as the message.
  • One caveat on the scorecard: the method was chosen from five candidates on this same history, so the shown improvement may be slightly optimistic.

Data: public Agmarknet daily mandi prices (Government of India open data), aggregated to weekly state medians. Prices are modal prices in Rs per quintal and mix varieties and grades. Forecasts are statistical projections for information only, not trading or financial advice. Built by VitastaIQ.
